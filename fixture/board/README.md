## What is already right

The part that has to be exact is the placement, and it is not laid out here at all: it is read
out of the DUT and carried through the fixture transform:

- **27 probes**, each at its DUT test point's own coordinates.
- **The outline and mounting holes**, taken from the DUT, so the two boards bolt together.
- Frame: origin `outline`, mirror `x`.
- Board: 100 x 70 mm.

## What is left to you

- **Routing.** A ratsnest and an accurate drill plan are the useful part; guessing trace paths is
  not. Autoroute it or draw it.
- **Placing the controller and any connectors.**
- **Filling the zones.** A GND pour is already drawn on both copper layers, held
  0.5 mm back from the edge. KiCad fills it on `Edit > Fill All Zones`.

These dimensions were transcribed from a catalogue and nobody has checked them against the supplier's own drawing. **Do that before you order**: series get revised, and a receptacle 0.1 mm fatter than this expects is a board that has to be redrilled.

The footprint is generated into `pinside.pretty/`, and `fp-lib-table` already points at it.

## Closing force

27 probes at 0.55 N each is **15 N** (1.5 kgf) at full travel.

A hand clamp or a couple of thumbscrews will close this. The load is small enough that the plate's own stiffness is what decides whether contact is even.

The DUT has no mounting holes, so there is nowhere to put a standoff. The plate has to be located some other way, and whatever locates it carries all of this.

## The controller

**Bare rp2350b on the fixture board.** The schematic does not draw it: add the MCU, its supply, crystal and USB, then wire the `FIX_*` nets.

## Mirroring

`mirror = x`. A fixture whose probes point up takes the DUT face-down,
which mirrors X. Get this wrong and the board is a perfect mirror image of the one you need, so
before ordering, print `pinside check <dut> -f svg` at 1:1 and lay the real board on it.
